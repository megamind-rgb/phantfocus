The short version

  • PHANTFOCUS keeps your focus history on your Mac. It never comes to us.
  • This website counts visits with GoatCounter. No cookies, no ads, no tracking across sites. We see totals, never who you are.
  • If you join the launch list, we keep your email address to send you one email when PHANTFOCUS launches, with your discount. Google Forms holds the list for us. Unsubscribe any time; we delete it at the latest 6 months after that email, or on 31 May 2027 if PHANTFOCUS hasn’t launched by then.
  • The app (the free preview, for people who have it) asks GitHub for updates. GitHub sees your IP address when it does. You can turn this off.
  • If you email us, we keep your email so we can reply.
  • We don’t sell data or use it for ads.

Who we are

We’re what data protection law calls the controller for this website and its visit counting, for the launch list, for the emails you send us, and for the app’s update checks. Your focus history stays on your Mac and never reaches us. GitHub is responsible for its own logs.

For anything about your data, use the contact address. We don’t have a data protection officer, and we aren’t required to have one.

This website

Hosting

GitHub Pages hosts this site. When your browser loads a page, GitHub, Inc. receives your IP address and the details every browser sends, like its name and version. GitHub keeps logs of this for security and decides how long. See GitHub’s Privacy Statement.

We host the site this way to run it simply and securely. That’s our legitimate interest (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)).

Counting visits

We count visits with GoatCounter. When a page loads, your browser runs GoatCounter’s small counting script, which this site keeps a copy of, so no other server is asked for it. The script sends GoatCounter the page’s address and title, the address of the page that linked you here, your screen’s width, and a check that helps it skip bots. Like any web request, this also carries your IP address and your browser’s name.

GoatCounter uses your IP address to work out your country. For up to 8 hours, and only in memory, it uses your IP address and browser to tell a new visit from a reload. It doesn’t store your IP address, and it doesn’t give you an ID. We’ve set it not to keep screen sizes, regions, languages or a list of single page views.

We see totals only: visits per page, which sites sent visitors, browsers and systems, and countries. When someone joins the launch list, that’s counted the same way, as one more total, never with the email address. The counter stores nothing on your device and follows you nowhere else. We keep the totals for 24 months.

Why: to know if people find the site and which pages help. Our legal basis is our legitimate interest in understanding, in a privacy-friendly way, how the site is used (Art. 6(1)(f)). Ask us and we’ll explain how we weighed this against your privacy.

GoatCounter does this for us, under our instructions, and keeps the data in the EU. See GoatCounter’s privacy policy.

You can say no to being counted

Use the switch below. It’s remembered in this browser with a small note, and that’s all the note is used for. If your browser sends Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track, we don’t load the counter at all. Blocking the script works too, and with JavaScript off nothing is counted.

Counting needs JavaScript. With it off, nothing is counted.

The app

This site offers no downloads until the launch. This part is for people who already have the free preview: what it does on your Mac.

What stays on your Mac

The app records your focus time only during a lock-in:

  • how long you stayed on your list, and how long the block screen was up,
  • how many times something not on your list was blocked,
  • the time per app or website on your list (anything else is only counted, never named),
  • each lock-in: when it started and ended, how long it was set for, and whether you finished.

It also keeps your settings: the apps and websites you picked, how long, your daily goal and how blocking looks.

All of this stays in ~/Library/Application Support/Generational Lockin, on your Mac. It’s never sent to us or anyone else.

What the app reads, and why

To block what’s not on your list, the app checks which app and browser tab is in front, about once a second during a lock-in. It uses two macOS permissions for this:

  • Accessibility lets it read the title of the window in front, and keep the pointer off the Dock while “Hide other apps” is on.
  • Automation lets it ask System Events which app is in front, and hide the Dock while “Hide other apps” is on (your Dock setting comes back afterwards). It also lets it ask your browser for the address of the tab in front. It reads addresses only while “Read website addresses” is on (it’s on unless you turn it off in Settings), and only in Safari, Chrome, Edge, Brave, Vivaldi, Chromium and Arc. While “Hide other apps” is on, it also asks each of these browsers that’s open, even one you aren’t looking at, for the tab it shows (its title and address), every 4 seconds. That way it can hide a browser that shows a website not on your list.

Window titles and full addresses stay in memory and are never written down. Only a website’s name, like wikipedia.org, is saved, and only for websites on your list. Outside a lock-in, the app reads none of this. None of it leaves your Mac.

You can take the permissions back any time in System Settings > Privacy & Security.

Updates

20 seconds after it opens, and then every 6 hours, the app asks GitHub for a small file that names the newest version. It does this also when it started at login with no window open. Each request carries your IP address. macOS adds the app’s name and build number, your macOS version and the languages your Mac prefers. GitHub tries to set its own cookies, one of them with an ID. The app turns them down and sends no cookies back. It sends no ID, account or information about how you use the app. Versions before 0.3.1 kept GitHub’s cookies in memory until the app quit, so GitHub could tell which checks came from the same running app.

GitHub receives this; we don’t. When you choose to install an update, the app downloads it from GitHub and checks its signature first.

Why: so you get fixes, including security fixes. That’s our legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)).

To stop automatic checks, choose PHANTFOCUS menu > Check for Updates Automatically. You can still check by hand.

Good to know

  • The first time you open the app, it asks whether to open when you log in. If you choose Open at Login, macOS tells you it added a login item. Change it any time in the app’s menu > Open at Login, or in System Settings > General > Login Items. (Versions before 0.3.1 turned it on at first launch without asking.)
  • The app has no account, no ads, no analytics and no crash reports.
  • Websites you open from the app, such as the websites on your list, open in your browser like any other link.

Deleting it

The app keeps each day until you delete it. To delete everything, quit PHANTFOCUS and turn off Open at Login. Then delete what the table above lists, starting with ~/Library/Application Support/Generational Lockin. Move the app to the Trash to remove it.

Your rights

You can ask for a copy of your data, and ask us to correct it, delete it, limit its use, or give it to you in a form you can take elsewhere. Use the contact address.

You can object at any time to anything we do based on legitimate interest: for example with the switch above, or by email. We’ll stop. You can withdraw your consent to the launch list the same way, at any time.

GoatCounter stores no IP address or visitor ID, so we can’t find your visits in the totals (GDPR Art. 11). Your focus history is on your Mac, not with us, so it’s yours to keep or delete.

You can complain to a data protection authority, for example where you live or work. We’d like the chance to fix things first, but you don’t have to ask us.

You never have to give us data. The site and the app work without it. Only an email reply, or the launch list, needs your email address.

Children

The site and the app are for everyone. We collect almost nothing from anyone, and nothing that tells us your age. The launch list needs your own consent, so please join it only if you’re 16 or older.

Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control

We honour both. If your browser sends either, we don’t load the counter, so your visit isn’t counted. No one else tracks you across sites through this website.
